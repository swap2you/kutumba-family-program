## 10–20 minute speaking script (deliverable prose)

> Facilitation note: Speak naturally. Pause for the 20-second pair share where indicated. Do not replace this with "see research."

Tonight we go one step deeper than last week. Week 2 said the body changes and the self continues. Week 3 asks what kind of self that is.

Essential question: If I am a soul, how should I live?

Primary: BG 2.20 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/20/

Teaching meaning: "The soul is never born and never dies; unborn, eternal, and everlasting — not destroyed when the body is destroyed."

Conclusion: The jīva is eternal, conscious, individual, minute, and related to Kṛṣṇa in service — not God Himself.

Please hear the is/is-not map clearly. The jīva is eternal, conscious, and individual. The jīva is a minute fragmental part related to Kṛṣṇa in service (BG 15.7). The jīva is **not** the temporary body. The jīva is **not** the Supreme Personality of Godhead.

If a child says "I am God," we smile and correct: "You are a dear spark meant to serve, not the whole sun."

Sun-and-ray, spark-from-fire, house-and-resident — all are pedagogy with limits. Rays are not the sun. Residents still care for the house.

Narrative support: Jaḍa Bharata's teaching context in ŚB 5.10 reminds us bodily status labels do not define the soul (https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/10/). Keep retellings gentle and nonviolent.

Science boundary for this week is explicit: **N/A — no empirical claim is used to prove or disprove ātman.** Laboratory methods measure bodies and behavior. If asked for science proof of the soul, say so out loud and defer doctrine to śāstra.

Case: neglecting hygiene "because we are soul." That is body contempt, not spirituality. Restore care as service readiness.

Supporting readings:
- BG 15.7 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/7/ — Living entities are eternal fragmental parts of Kṛṣṇa — qualitative likeness, quantitative difference.
- BG 2.17 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/17/ — That which pervades the body is indestructible.
- ŚB 5.10 (Jaḍa Bharata context) — https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/10/ — Narrative support: soul beyond bodily status labels — gentle paraphrase only.

Memory: I am an eternal soul — conscious, individual, and meant for Kṛṣṇa's service.

I am going to teach the analogies slowly, with limits in the same breath, because an analogy without a limit becomes a new false doctrine.

First: **Sun and sunray**. Ray shares light-nature with the sun but is not the sun. Limit: Ray is not the sun — blocks 'I am God.'

Second: **Spark from fire**. Same nature, dependent existence. Limit: Not independent Godhood; dependence remains.

Third: **House and resident**. Resident is not the house; still cares for the house. Limit: Can feel impersonal if overused — keep personhood of the jīva clear.

Hold those as teaching tools, not as replacements for BG 2.20.

Now two sourced examples, paraphrased only.

Example A — Jaḍa Bharata and King Rahūgaṇa (ŚB 5.9–5.10). Bodily labels (carrier, king, appearance) do not define the soul. Classroom retelling must be nonviolent and symbolic; no invented dialogue. Link: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/10/

Example B — Fragmental part teaching (BG 15.7). Supports individuality + relationship to Kṛṣṇa without claiming Supreme identity. Link: https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/7/

Next I will walk all three household cases out loud so you can copy the pattern at home. Please listen for seven fields every time: situation, mistaken conclusion, principle, source, compassionate response, action, and what not to say.

Case 1.
Situation: A child proudly says, 'I am God.'
Mistaken conclusion: Soul equals the Supreme.
Principle and source: BG 15.7 — eternal fragmental part; BG 2.20 — eternal individual self. (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/7/)
Compassionate response: Smile; correct gently: we are tiny parts meant to serve, not the whole.
Family action: Practice the sentence: 'I am a soul who serves Kṛṣṇa.'
What not to say: You're blasphemous. Shame the child publicly.

Case 2.
Situation: An adult dismisses soul talk as anti-science.
Mistaken conclusion: Science has disproved the soul.
Principle and source: Different domains: empiricism measures bodies/behavior; śāstra teaches ātman. (Program science boundary + BG 2.20)
Compassionate response: Agree that labs measure physical processes. Clarify we are not claiming a lab proof of ātman tonight.
Family action: Offer the deferral line; invite reading the verse URL later.
What not to say: Scientists are demons. Faith means never asking questions.

Case 3.
Situation: A family neglects sleep and hygiene 'because we are soul.'
Mistaken conclusion: Body contempt is spiritual.
Principle and source: Care without identity confusion — resident cares for house. (Pedagogy limit of house analogy + prior W2 care theme)
Compassionate response: Restore basic care as service readiness, not as identity.
Family action: Pick one body-care act (sleep time / water / bathing) as service this week.
What not to say: The body is illusion so ignore doctors. Only weak people rest.

I will now ask the room three discovery questions. You may answer in a whisper to a partner:
- What does 'eternal' mean in kid words?
- Where do people confuse 'soul' with 'I am God'?
- What is one way a soul shows service at home?

Understanding check — please be ready to say in one sentence: The jīva is eternal, conscious, individual, minute, and related to Kṛṣṇa in service — not God Himself.. Name the primary: BG 2.20. Name what we are not teaching tonight: ranking, speculation, and the misconception “All souls are God / we are the Supreme.”.

Application — before you leave the shared opening, decide silently: time + place for home practice; who starts if others are tired; and the minimum version if the week is hard. Five minutes counts. Memory line plus one kind action counts.

Let me demonstrate a minimum home practice out loud so nobody leaves confused. Tonight after dinner, or at the same chair each evening, someone says the memory line once: “I am an eternal soul — conscious, individual, and meant for Kṛṣṇa's service..” Then one family member paraphrases BG 2.20 in a single sentence. Then one gratitude or service act — thanking someone, offering water, putting shoes away as kindness, or a short prayer. If the house is chaotic, keep only the memory line and one kind action. That still honors tonight’s conclusion: The jīva is eternal, conscious, individual, minute, and related to Kṛṣṇa in service — not God Himself.

When we reunite at 3:10, I will not score families. I will ask for one sentence: “This week we will…” Drawings count. Whispered parent sentences count. Loud brilliance is not holier than quiet sincerity.

Likely questions you may hear from adults — rehearse these answers with me:

If someone asks, “Are we God?” — answer: No. We are eternal fragmental parts meant for service (BG 15.7).

If someone asks, “Can science prove the soul?” — answer: N/A as proof. Empiricism does not establish ātman; we teach from śāstra.

If someone asks, “Is the soul created at birth?” — answer: BG 2.20 teaches the soul is never born and never dies — paraphrase carefully; avoid speculative cosmology debates.

If someone asks, “Do animals have souls?” — answer: Gauḍīya teaching affirms life in many forms; keep class focused on human family practice tonight without species contempt.

If someone asks, “What should I *do* if I am a soul?” — answer: Live as a servant of Kṛṣṇa: hear, remember, serve, speak respectfully — start with this week's home cue.
