# C1-W2 Older Teacher Guide (Grades 4–5)

**Week:** I Am Not This Body  
**Primary:** BG 2.13 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/  
**Session length:** ~40 minutes (parallel track ~2:30–3:10)  
**Parents:** Remain onsite; reunite at 3:10  
**Status:** Internal founding-cohort teaching material — human/temple review EXTERNAL_OPEN

---

## Objective

Students observe BG 2.13, explain that the body changes while the conscious self continues, map Care-without-confusion, practice respectful body speech, and contribute one project sentence answering: **How should knowing I am not only this body change how we speak about bodies?**

## Exact memory phrase

> My body changes; I continue as the conscious self.

**Short echo:** Care for the body; don’t confuse it with the self.

## Teacher background

- **KUTUMBA teaching meaning:** Just as the embodied self passes through childhood, youth, and old age in one body, the sober person is not bewildered when the self passes to another body. (Label as KUTUMBA teaching meaning — not as BBT translation.)
- **Supporting frames (one sentence each):** BG 2.22 — garments analogy with limits (clothes chosen; bodies not costumes for mockery); BG 2.20 — preview only (deep teaching next week).
- **Block:** “Psychology or photos prove the soul” and “Body neglect is spiritual” and “Appearance jokes are harmless.”
- **Scope:** No full jīva ontology dump (W3); no rarity scare (W4).

## Minute-by-minute plan

### 0–5 min — Settle and hook

1. Welcome; feet on floor.
2. Hook: “Which is worse as a teaching error — treating the body as the whole self, or treating the body as trash?” Let two volunteers answer; hold both as incomplete extremes.
3. State essential question: How should knowing I am not only this body change how we speak about bodies?

### 5–14 min — Primary text observation (BG 2.13)

1. Display verse; read IAST aloud together once; teacher reads Devanāgarī slowly or plays authorized audio if available.
2. Students underline three idea-clusters in the teaching meaning: **childhood–youth–old age**, **embodied self**, **not bewildered**.
3. Observation prompts (write short answers):
   - What changes inside one body according to the verse?
   - Who is not bewildered when passage continues?
   - What does this verse **not** authorize? (mockery; body contempt; lab proof of ātman)
4. Add one-sentence bridges:
   - BG 2.22 — garments support continuity teaching; limits: bodies deserve care; not fashion props for jokes.
   - BG 2.20 — preview only: soul not slain when body is — deep teaching next week.
5. Paraphrase only; no invented Kṛṣṇa–Arjuna dialogue; no gore.

### 14–22 min — Worksheet questions

Students complete Section A of the older activity pack:
1. Restate KUTUMBA teaching meaning in their own words (one sentence).
2. Name two body changes everyone recognizes.
3. Name one way to care for the body as service readiness (not identity).
4. Why don’t photos **prove** the soul?
5. Why is “bodies don’t matter” not this week’s teaching?
6. Rewrite “It’s just a joke” into a repair sentence after appearance teasing.

Teacher circulates; use `OLDER-ANSWER-KEY.md` for expected directions (not for grading ranks).

### 22–28 min — Scenario cards (small groups)

Each group gets one fictional card:

**Card A — Sibling appearance tease at snack:** Mistaken line: “Just a joke.”  
**Card B — Teen identity = fitness metrics:** Mistaken line: “The body project *is* the self.”  
**Card C — Relative asks weight publicly:** Mistaken line: “Public body talk is fine.”

For each card, group writes:
1. Mistaken conclusion
2. Principle from BG 2.13 (one sentence)
3. Compassionate response
4. Better family action
5. One sentence they should **not** say (shame, ranking, or public exposure)

Share one insight per group (60 seconds).

### 28–33 min — Puzzle / game: Respectful Fork

1. Teacher reads a short fork aloud (two endings about body speech or care).
2. Teams hold up **R** (respectful / aligned) or **M** (mistaken).
3. Three rounds, then students explain one “R” choice using the words care or self continues.
4. Keep energy respectful; no mocking wrong holds.

Sample forks (paraphrase freely):
- Tease vs repair after a height comment
- Skip sleep “because spiritual” vs rest as service readiness
- Announce someone’s stats vs change the subject warmly

### 33–37 min — Diagram task

On the Care–Self sheet, students fill:

`Body stages (child→youth→elder) → Conscious self continues → Kind speech + responsible care`

Circle the **Kind speech** box and write the short echo inside it. Use a low-risk example (shoes too small, lost pencil joke) — not forced trauma disclosure.

### 37–40 min — Project contribution + reflection + handoff

1. Complete project half-sheet: “This week our family will speak about bodies by _______________.”
2. Reflection (one sentence): How does BG 2.13 challenge both body-as-self and body-contempt?
3. Cleanup; bring sheets to reunification.
4. Parent handoff line: Home practice is once daily — notice one body change or repair one speech habit; say the memory phrase.

## Extension (if group finishes early)

Compare garment analogy limits with driver–vehicle: maintain the vehicle for service; refuse harsh dualism. Still no W3 “I am God” deep dive beyond a one-line preview if asked.
