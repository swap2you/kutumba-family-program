## 5–12 — BG 2.13 observation (source)

**Source:** https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/

**Devanāgarī:** देहिनोऽस्मिन् यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा । तथा देहान्तरप्राप्तिर्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥ १३ ॥

**IAST:**  
`dehino ’smin yathā dehe kaumāraṁ yauvanaṁ jarā / tathā dehāntara-prāptir dhīras tatra na muhyati`

**KUTUMBA teaching meaning (not a BBT translation):**  
Just as the embodied self passes through childhood, youth, and old age in one body, the sober person is not bewildered when the self passes to another body.

Say in plain language:
- Body stages change; the conscious self continues.
- Care for the body remains a duty; identity is not reduced to appearance or metrics.
- Photos and psychology can help respectful teaching — they do **not** prove the soul.
- Deep jīva ontology waits for W3.

Adult memory line:
> My body changes; I continue as the conscious self.

## 12–20 — Respectful language card sort (P02)

**Printable:** P02  

Steps:
1. Lay out two mats: **RESPECTFUL** and **MISTAKEN**.
2. Adults sort language cards (appearance jokes, redirects, care statements, contempt statements).
3. Discuss one “almost right” card that needs a limit (e.g., health talk that becomes identity talk).

Keep tone practical, not competitive. No ranking of families’ past speech.

Facilitator key direction:
- Respectful = dignity + care + privacy + repair.
- Mistaken = mockery + public stats + body-as-trash + “just a joke” after hurt.

## 20–30 — Substantial family case (P03)

**Printable:** P03  

**Case (fictional):** A middle-school child comes home upset after a relative joked about their height at a gathering. A sibling laughs and repeats the joke at dinner. One parent says “toughen up”; another parent freezes.

Ask:
1. What is the tempting mistaken conclusion?
2. What principle from BG 2.13 applies?
3. What is a compassionate response that protects dignity without public shaming?
4. What is one seven-day household action?
5. What should we **not** say?

Suggested facilitator answer direction:
- Mistaken: body jokes are harmless; silence is kindness enough.
- Principle: body changes; conscious person continues; speech must honor dignity (BG 2.13).
- Response: stop the repeat; affirm the child; privately coach the sibling; prepare a warm redirect for relatives.
- Action: family speech pledge — no appearance jokes this week; one nightly memory-line cue.
- Do not say: toughen up; spiritual people ignore feelings; announce the child’s stats.
