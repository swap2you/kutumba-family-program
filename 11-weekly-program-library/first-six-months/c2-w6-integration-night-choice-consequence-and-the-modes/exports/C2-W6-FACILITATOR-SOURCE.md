Now the Prem-kī-Kathā case — fictional, for learning only. Please listen for the five lenses.

A family has a recurring Friday problem. Everyone arrives late. One parent blames traffic. The children blame hunger. Another adult says, “This is our karma; nothing can change.” Devices stay active. No one prepared food on time. By the time kīrtana begins, irritation has already spread.

Pause twenty seconds with a partner: Which lens do you notice first?

I will now walk the five lenses aloud so you can copy the pattern later in the lab.

Lens 1 — Action and likely reaction: Late prep, active devices, and rushed arrival are actions. Irritation before kīrtana is a likely reaction. BG 3.9 reminds us that action has consequence; offering and responsible preparation matter.

Lens 2 — Choice / control: Traffic may be influenced but not fully controlled. Device timers, dinner prep start time, and leaving earlier are more controllable. BG 18.63 centers tonight: deliberate, then choose. Fatalism that “nothing can change” is a misconception to block.

Lens 3 — Conditioning / modes: Rush, irritation, and restless screens often feel passion-like; dull “whatever” can feel ignorance-like; a calm plan can feel goodness-like. BG 14.5: modes bind — but we describe **conditions**, not brand people. Mode-labeling people is blocked.

Lens 4 — False promise / attention loop: “One more scroll and then we’ll leave” can decorate the prison cell of lateness. BG 7.14: the loop is hard to cross without shelter and boundaries.

Lens 5 — Responsible action + shelter: Choose one redesign — earlier prep cue, device basket, snack ready, choice-pause at the door — and take shelter: a short prayer, memory line, or soft kīrtana start that does not shame latecomers. Victim-blaming is blocked; compassion redesigns the system.
