## 5–10 — ŚB 1.2.18

**Source:** https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/  

**IAST:**
`naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣu nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā / bhagavaty uttama-śloke bhaktir bhavati naiṣṭhikī`

**KUTUMBA teaching meaning (not a BBT translation):**
When we regularly hear and serve the Bhāgavata (book and devotee association), troubles in the heart are cleared and steady devotion to the Lord becomes established.

Say in plain language:
- **nityaṁ** = regular / steady rhythm;
- book Bhāgavata and devotee association support each other;
- Saturday attendance is useful; home practice helps attendance become transformative;
- do not claim weekly attendance alone equals transformation;
- no long purport lecture.

Memory line for adults:
> Regular hearing and service cleanse the heart and make devotion steady.

## 18–28 — Two-family case study

**Printable:** P03  

**Family A:** Attends every Saturday, enjoys the group, but does nothing together at home.  
**Family B:** Reads/chants at home but gradually stops joining temple/devotee association because “home practice is enough.”

Ask:
1. What is healthy in each family?
2. What is missing?
3. What is one nonjudgmental repair?
4. How can KUTUMBA support rather than replace temple life?

Suggested balanced answer (presenter only):
- A has association/rhythm but lacks home watering of the garden.
- B has private practice but risks isolation from devotee association and service.
- Repair is rebalancing — not shaming: protect Saturday *and* a tiny home practice; keep temple/association as friendship and service, not optional “extra.”

## 35–40 — First saṅkalpa

**Printable:** P05  

Formula:
**specific action + frequency + trigger + minimum version**

Example (may read aloud):
> After dinner on Tuesday and Thursday, we will sit for five minutes, offer one prayer, chant three mahā-mantras, and each say one appreciation. On a hard day, minimum version is one prayer and one mahā-mantra.

Rules:
- Keep private.
- No photo proof.
- No leaderboard.
- Minimum version counts as success.
