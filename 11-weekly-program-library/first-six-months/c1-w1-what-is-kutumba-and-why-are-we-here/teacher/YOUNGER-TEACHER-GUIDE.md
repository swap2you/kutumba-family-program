## Objective

Children practice four family habits — **HEAR · CHANT · SERVE · RESPECT** — plant a Bhakti Garden flower, and take home a memory badge. They can say the child-facing memory phrase aloud.

## Exact memory phrase (child-facing)

> Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.

## Teacher background (2 minutes to review)

- **KUTUMBA teaching meaning (adult wording):** When we regularly hear and serve the Bhāgavata (book and devotee association), troubles in the heart are cleared and steady devotion to the Lord becomes established.
- **Child-facing bridge:** When our family hears about Kṛṣṇa, says His names, helps with love, and uses kind words, we help one another remember Kṛṣṇa — like a garden that grows when we care for it again and again.
- **Block misconceptions (child words):**  
  - “Coming on Saturday is enough; we can skip home.”  
  - “Only big kids / only temple people hear.”
- **Do not:** invent sage dialogue; use fear; rank who “hears better”; claim science proves devotion; claim our room equals Naimiṣāraṇya.

## Minute-by-minute executable plan (40 minutes)

### 0–3 min — Rules

1. Welcome by name; feet on floor; hands in lap.
2. Teach four house signals in child words:  
   - **Safe body** — feet on the floor.  
   - **Kind words** — soft voices.  
   - **Freeze** — stop when the teacher says freeze.  
   - **Ask first** — before touching what is not yours.
3. Point to the calm corner: “Quiet reset is okay; then we rejoin.”
4. Echo memory phrase once together: *Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.*

### 3–8 min — Story (source-grounded paraphrase only)

**Say this spine (do not invent deity dialogue):**

> Long ago, wise people gathered in a peaceful place to hear about the Lord and ask how to live well. They did not come only once and forget. They came ready to listen carefully.  
> We are not those forest sages — but we can borrow their *habit*: set a protected time, hear together, say Kṛṣṇa’s names, help with love, and use kind words.  
> Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam teaches that when we regularly hear and serve — the book and the friendship of devotees — the heart can become steadier in devotion to the Lord.  
> A garden grows when someone cares for it again and again. One splash and then forgetting is not enough. Saturday hearing *and* a tiny home practice belong together.  
> Our memory line: **Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.**

**Story boundaries:** Paraphrase from ŚB 1.1–1.2 assembly pattern and ŚB 1.2.18 themes only. No invented quotes. No fear. No claiming our room equals Naimiṣāraṇya.

### 8–15 min — Four Corners (Y01 + Y02)

1. Show the four corner signs from **Y01**: HEAR · CHANT · SERVE · RESPECT. Read each short line aloud.
2. Practice walk: “Everyone point to HEAR… now walk gently to HEAR… freeze… return to circle.”
3. Read scenario cards from **Y02** one at a time (use 6–8 cards if time is tight; keep the rest for extension).
4. Children walk to the matching corner. Affirm effort. Use the Y02 answer key quietly.
5. If two corners seem possible, honor the thinking, then guide to the best match.
6. End seated; echo memory phrase once.

### 15–27 min — Bhakti Garden craft (Y03)

1. Give each child **Y03** Bhakti Garden worksheet.
2. Teacher models: color the **center** first — *Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.*
3. Color four petals: **HEAR · CHANT · SERVE · RESPECT** (large crayon areas).
4. Bottom line: with a helper if needed, fill *This week our family will grow:* with one small idea (example: “chant three times after dinner”).
5. Optional sticker on one petal. Keep dignity; do not compare gardens.

### 27–33 min — Movement / memory echo

1. Children walk gently in a circle (or march in place).
2. Teacher says **Freeze!** — everyone stops.
3. Teacher starts: “Our family helps…” — children finish: “…one another remember Kṛṣṇa.”
4. Teacher says **Grow!** — children show four fingers: Hear, Chant, Serve, Respect (one per finger) then soft clap.
5. Three rounds. On round three, point to Y01 signs in order while children echo each word.
6. Sit. Optional 60-second calm if energy spikes (use **Y05** one-card redirect only if needed).

### 33–38 min — Memory badge / bookmark (Y04)

1. Give **Y04** badge cut-out: **WE HELP EACH OTHER REMEMBER KṚṢṆA**.
2. Children color; helpers cut/punch; add yarn.
3. Glue or place the four mini-icon labels (HEAR · CHANT · SERVE · RESPECT) on the back or bookmark strip.
4. Practice once: hold badge and say the memory phrase together.

### 38–40 min — Cleanup and reunification handoff

1. Crayons in bin; scraps in recycle; signs stay up until parents arrive if helpful.
2. Each child holds garden sheet (**Y03**) and badge (**Y04**).
3. At **3:10** reunification, child-facing share line:  
   > Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.
4. Parent handoff (one sentence, Saturday afternoon / at-home language):  
   > Memory phrase: “Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.” At home after dinner dishes, say it once and notice one petal: Hear, Chant, Serve, or Respect.
5. Do not report private behavior struggles in front of other families.

## Behavior redirects (quick reference)

| Signal | Teacher line | Child action |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Running | “Freeze bodies — garden walk.” | Freeze, then walk |
| Snatching | “Kind hands — ask first.” | Hands to lap |
| Loud shout | “Soft garden voice.” | Soft repeat |
| Shutdown | “Quiet reset is okay.” | Calm corner 60 sec |

Optional: sort one **Y05** card as a class redirect model (Safe / Needs a reset) — never shame the child who needed the reset.

## Backup low-prep (if craft fails or energy crashes)

1. Sit in a circle with soft toss object.
2. Child catches, says one word: “hear,” “chant,” “serve,” “respect,” or “Kṛṣṇa,” then tosses to next.
3. End with three echoes of: *Our family helps one another remember Kṛṣṇa.*

## What success looks like

- Child can walk to at least two correct Four Corners.
- Child can echo (or finish) the memory phrase.
- Child takes home Y03 garden and/or Y04 badge.

## Boundaries

- Snack/water only happens in main schedule (**3:30**), not as a track bribe.
- No weekly meal language.
- No fabricated approval stickers implying temple certification.
- Privacy: no forced confession of “we never practice at home.”
- Use Saturday afternoon / at-home language for handoff (after dinner dishes), not evening-only wording.
