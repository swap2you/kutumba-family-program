# C2-W5 Older Teacher Guide (Grades 4–5)

**Track length:** ~40 minutes (Saturday 2:35–3:10)  
**Essential question:** Where are we decorating bondage?  
**Primary:** BG 7.14 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/14/  
**Conclusion students should state:** Māyā is the Lord’s hard-to-overcome material energy made of the modes; surrender/shelter crosses it; we do not hate the world, scorn duties, or call women māyā.

---

## Objective

Students will (1) observe BG 7.14 on VedaBase and paraphrase the KUTUMBA teaching meaning, (2) sort scenario cards into Decorating / Shelter / Misuse-to-correct without labeling persons as māyā, (3) complete a diagram and worksheet, and (4) write one reunification sentence plus a three-day boundary idea.

## Teacher background

- Teaching meaning: This divine illusory energy made of the modes is hard to overcome — but those who surrender to the Lord cross beyond it.
- Supports: BG 2.62–2.63 (attention cascade), BG 15.7 (eternal fragment struggling with senses—compassion), BG 9.10 (nature under Kṛṣṇa’s direction—blocks world-hatred).
- Misconceptions to interrupt immediately: women are māyā; world is fake/evil; householder duty is contemptible; addiction confession pressure among peers.

## Minute-by-minute

### 0–5 — Hook

Show three captions: (1) phone during Gītā reading, (2) endless house-upgrade talk delaying chanting, (3) someone calling a girl “māyā.” Ask: “Which are problems—and which sentence must we correct?” Collect condition/choice words only. Post essential question: **Where are we decorating bondage?** Clarify: decorating bondage ≠ “your house is a jail.”

### 5–12 — Primary text observation

Students open or view BG 7.14 and complete four lines in notebooks:

1. **Setting:** Kṛṣṇa teaching Arjuna in the Gītā (Chapter 7).
2. **Key Sanskrit/IAST phrase they notice** (e.g., *mama māyā duratyayā* or *prapadyante* / *taranti*).
3. **Paraphrase (KUTUMBA teaching meaning):** This divine illusory energy made of the modes is hard to overcome — but those who surrender to the Lord cross beyond it.
4. **What it does *not* say:** That women *are* māyā; that the world is fake/evil; that householders should scorn duties.

Teacher checks paraphrases against answer key; corrects invented verse numbers.

### 12–18 — Worksheet questions (from activity pack)

Students answer:

- How BG 2.62–2.63 describe a cascade from contemplation to fall (own words).
- How BG 9.10 blocks world-hatred talk.
- One three-day media/consumption boundary (private, kind).

### 18–26 — Scenario cards + boundary_sort puzzle

In pairs, sort six short scenarios into three columns: Decorating bondage / Shelter practice / Misuse to correct.  
**Rule card on table:** “Never write a classmate’s name as ‘māyā.’”  
Teacher circulates; flips any “she is māyā” talk into explicit correction.  
One group shares a sort aloud; class may challenge kindly with evidence from BG 7.14 sense.

### 26–32 — Diagram task

On the concept diagram, label:

- Māyā: Lord’s energy / modes / hard to overcome
- Arrow to “surrender → cross (BG 7.14)”
- Supports: attention cascade (2.62–2.63); nature under Lord (9.10); eternal part struggles (15.7)
- Red “X” on “women are māyā” and “hate the world”

### 32–36 — Project contribution + reflection

- Write one Attention Boundary card: trigger · rule · shelter · three days.
- Reflection sentence: “An analogy (prison cell / shiny cage) helps me picture misplaced investment, but scripture is the authority because…”
- Extension (fast finishers): Write a respectful correction sentence for Case 3 (women misuse).

### 36–40 — Reunification sentence + cleanup

Each student drafts one sentence to share upstairs, e.g.:  
“Māyā is hard; we take shelter; we don’t call people māyā or hate our homes.”  
Stack materials; teacher keeps answer key private.

## Answer key pointer

Use `activities/OLDER-ANSWER-KEY.md` for paraphrase targets and reject lists. Do not distribute the key to students.

## Behavior / speech ethics

- Interrupt “women are māyā” in one calm, firm sentence.
- No confession pressure about screens, shopping, or vices.
- Privacy: no ranking families’ “māyā scores.”
- Prison-cell analogy only with the stated limit (≠ hatred of home).

## Backup if tech fails

Teacher reads IAST aloud from verse card; students write paraphrase from dictation; card sort continues offline.
