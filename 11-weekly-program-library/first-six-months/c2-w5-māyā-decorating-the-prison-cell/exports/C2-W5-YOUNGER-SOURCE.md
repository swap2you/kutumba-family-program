# C2-W5 Younger Teacher Guide (K–2)

**Track length:** ~40 minutes (within Saturday 2:35–3:10 window)  
**Theme:** Shiny things can pull attention; we practice taking shelter with Kṛṣṇa.  
**Primary:** BG 7.14 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/14/  
**Hard block:** Never call a child, parent, or friend “māyā.” Never say a girl or woman is māyā. Never teach that home is a bad jail.

---

## Objective

By the end of the track, each child can (1) name the difference between a “shiny pull” and a “shelter choice,” (2) join the Boundary Basket game, and (3) say the memory phrase without labeling people.

## Exact memory phrase

> When shiny things call, I take shelter with Kṛṣṇa.

*(Adult memory line differs; children use this actionable phrase.)*

## Teacher background (2 minutes private prep)

- BG 7.14 teaches that the Lord’s divine material energy made of the modes is hard to overcome, but those who surrender to the Lord cross beyond it.
- For K–2, translate to: shiny screens, toys, and snacks can pull attention; we practice a short boundary and soft chanting as shelter.
- Block: calling people māyā; world-hatred; house-is-jail scare talk; forcing children to confess “bad screen habits.”
- Story must paraphrase only; no invented deity dialogue; prison-cell image for adults only—use “shiny pull vs shelter” with children.

## Materials

- Two picture cards: “Shiny pull” (glowing device icon) and “Shelter” (beads / quiet sitting)
- Soft basket labeled “Phone/toy sleeps here” (demo only)
- Soft toss object (beanbag)
- Printed `visuals/V12/line-art-younger.svg` or `.png` (US Letter) + crayons
- Take-home card stock (memory phrase inside)
- Timer; tissues; water

---

## Minute-by-minute run of show

### 0–5 — Opening / recall

1. Circle sit; feet on floor.
2. Ask: “Who remembers a calm habit that helps hearing?” (one or two shares from last week if recalled).
3. Show shiny-pull card and shelter card. Children echo: shiny pull / shelter.
4. Say: “Today we learn that shiny things can call our eyes—and we can take shelter with Kṛṣṇa.”

### 5–13 — Story script (paraphrase; ~5–8 minutes spoken)

**Title:** *The Shiny Call and the Quiet Corner*

Teacher narrates (no invented deity dialogue):

> In one house lived a family who loved Kṛṣṇa’s names.  
> One evening, a child sat to listen to a short verse. Then a shiny screen beeped. Eyes jumped. Ears forgot the verse.  
> A helper adult said kindly: “The shiny thing called. We can pause it. We can put it to sleep in the basket.”  
> They placed the device in the basket, sat in the quiet corner, and softly answered the holy name. Hearing felt clearer.  
> Another day, someone almost said a mean nickname about a friend. The helper said: “We never call people māyā. Māyā is a big grown-up word for a covering energy. Friends are people we respect.”  
> Remember: when shiny things call, I take shelter with Kṛṣṇa—and I speak kindly about people.

Pause once mid-story for children to hold up shiny-pull or shelter card.

### 13–16 — Three wonder questions

1. What was new in the story about the shiny call?
2. Whom can we serve when we choose the quiet corner?
3. When can our family practice a short “basket sleep” before chanting?

Accept short answers; redirect labels (“My sister is māyā”) to “We never call people that. We talk about shiny pulls and shelter.”

### 16–24 — Movement game: Boundary Basket (steps)

1. Place one soft demo “phone” or toy near the circle and an empty soft basket.
2. On “Shiny call!” children look at the shiny card, then freeze hands on knees (no grabbing).
3. On “Boundary!” one volunteer (or whole group taking turns) gently places the soft demo item in the basket.
4. On “Shelter!” children sit tall and softly echo memory phrase once.
5. Repeat two rounds; end with whole group seated, soft echo of memory phrase twice.
6. Teacher states: “We practiced a boundary. We did not call anyone a bad name.”

### 24–32 — Craft / printable task (steps)

1. Give each child the younger line-art page.
2. Children color a quiet corner or listening scene; optionally add a small basket.
3. Fold card stock; child (or teacher scribe) writes/copies: **When shiny things call, I take shelter with Kṛṣṇa.**
4. On the back, draw one sleeping device/toy in a basket OR one soft chant mouth.
5. If time: pass soft object; each child says one kind word about a friend, then the memory phrase.

### 32–35 — Verse / recall

- Teacher holds verse card and says slowly: “Grown-ups remember Bhagavad-gītā 7.14: the Lord’s covering energy is hard to cross, but shelter with the Lord helps. Our words are the memory phrase.”
- Children repeat memory phrase twice.
- Optional echo: “Shiny pulls happen. Shelter is a choice.”

### 35–40 — Cleanup and parent handoff

1. Crayons away; papers named; floor check.
2. Behavior redirects if needed: feet on floor · kind words · toy rests · quiet reset corner (30 seconds), then rejoin.
3. **Parent handoff script:**  
   > Today’s phrase: “When shiny things call, I take shelter with Kṛṣṇa.”  
   > Home cue: one media/toy boundary for three days, kindly—not as punishment theater.  
   > Please never call children or women “māyā.”

## Behavior redirects

| Signal | Redirect |
| --- | --- |
| Rough bodies | “Feet on floor; hands soft.” |
| Calling someone māyā / mean nicknames | “We name shiny pulls, not friends.” |
| House-is-jail scare talk | “Homes can be kind places. We practice shelter.” |
| Overwhelm / confession pressure | Quiet reset: sit with teacher, three breaths, rejoin. Pass is always OK. |

## Backup (if craft fails / time collapses)

Pass soft object around the circle. Each child: (1) one kind word, (2) echo memory phrase. End with one Boundary Basket round.

## What success looks like

- Children can point to shiny-pull vs shelter without labeling peers.
- Most children join basket boundary and seated phrase echo.
- No child is publicly ranked or forced to confess screen use.
