# C2-W5 Case Studies

**Week:** Māyā: Decorating the Prison Cell
**Primary principle source:** BG 7.14 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/14/
**Note:** All cases are fictional / anonymized teaching constructs for the founding cohort. Not reports about real cohort families.

---

### Constructed case 1 — Notification spiral during reading (fictional)

- **Situation:** A parent sits to read Bhagavad-gītā. A notification appears. “Ten seconds,” the mind says. Twenty minutes later, messages, news, and shopping have replaced the verse.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** “I can beat this with more willpower alone,” or “Screens are absolute evil so I must hate all media forever.”
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 7.14 — hard to overcome; surrender/shelter crosses. BG 2.62–63 attention chain.
- **Compassionate response:** Name the false promise without shame. Set one boundary (phone in another room for the reading window). Pair with shelter: five minutes japa or one verse card before any unlock.
- **Family action:** Choose one three-day media boundary and one shelter cue; write both on the fridge.
- **What not to say:** “You’re a fake devotee.” Public screen-time ranking. Blaming a spouse’s gender for māyā.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — shiny card vs helping card. Older — write false promise → boundary → shelter.
### Constructed case 2 — Shopping as identity polish (fictional)

- **Situation:** A family already has needed clothes. A sale notification triggers a cart fill “to feel put together for Sunday.” Debt stress and comparison follow.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** “Decorating the house/wardrobe is the spiritual project,” or “Wanting beauty is always sinful.”
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 7.14 crossing by surrender; prison-cell analogy as pedagogy for misplaced ultimate projects.
- **Compassionate response:** Separate stewardship (needed care) from image-as-salvation. Delay purchase 24 hours; offer the saved attention to a short kīrtana or service chore.
- **Family action:** One consumption delay rule for three days; replace one browse session with a shelter practice.
- **What not to say:** Shaming someone for poverty or for owning nice things. Calling women “māyā.”
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — “need vs shiny want” sort. Older — list one stewardship buy vs one image buy.
### Constructed case 3 — Mislabeling a person as māyā (fictional)

- **Situation:** After a conflict, a parent mutters that a child, spouse, or “women in general” are māyā.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** People (especially women) are māyā; therefore contempt is spiritual.
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 7.14 — māyā is the Lord’s energy of the modes, not a person-class. Living beings are to be respected; cover is energetic, not an excuse for hate.
- **Compassionate response:** Correct firmly and kindly: people are not māyā. Māyā is the energy that makes us forget Kṛṣṇa. Repair the relationship; return to surrender and shelter.
- **Family action:** Family correction card: “People ≠ māyā.” Practice one respectful repair sentence.
- **What not to say:** Anything that scapegoats gender, age, or a child as the illusory energy itself.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — clear sentence drill. Older — rewrite the mistaken sentence into a true one.
