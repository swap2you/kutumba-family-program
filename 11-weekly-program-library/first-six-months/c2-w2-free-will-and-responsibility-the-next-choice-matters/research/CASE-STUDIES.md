# C2-W2 Case Studies

**Week:** Free Will and Responsibility: The Next Choice Matters  
**Primary principle source:** BG 18.63 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/63/  
**Note:** All cases are fictional / anonymized teaching constructs for the founding cohort. Not reports about real cohort families.

---

### Constructed case 1 — Sibling anger pause (fictional)

- **Situation:** Two siblings argue over a shared controller. One feels heat in the chest and lifts a hand to snatch and shout.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** “I had no choice — my anger made me do it.”
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 18.63 — deliberate, then act; BG 3.34 — do not come under control of attachment/aversion (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/34/).
- **Compassionate response:** Name the feeling without shame; invite a three-second pause; remember Kṛṣṇa and the family rule; choose ask / take turns / get help.
- **Family action:** Place red–yellow–green cards near the shared-device shelf; practice Pause–Remember–Choose once after dinner.
- **What not to say:** “Bad devotee.” Public shaming. Comparing siblings’ spirituality.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — traffic-light cards only. Older — write mistaken conclusion vs next responsible choice.

### Constructed case 2 — Homework versus game (fictional)

- **Situation:** Homework is unfinished when a game invite or screen urge arrives. The parent is tired and tempted either to explode or to abandon the evening structure.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** “Responsible choice means whatever feels easiest,” or “schedules own us so effort is pointless.”
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 18.63 — full deliberation before action; home practice is one chosen trigger, not perfectionism.
- **Compassionate response:** Sort control circles (invite and fatigue may be given; ten focused minutes are choosable); pick a minimum duty block then a timed break.
- **Family action:** Make homework-start the week’s Pause–Remember–Choose trigger.
- **What not to say:** Spiritual threats; ranking children by grades inside KUTUMBA circle.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — “work card first, then play card.” Older — map event → urge → choice → consequence.

### Constructed case 3 — Parent snapping, then repairing (fictional)

- **Situation:** During a time-pressure transition (shoes, keys), a parent snaps harshly. Regret arrives within a minute.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** “Parents have no agency under stress,” or “one snap proves I am hopeless, so why try.”
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 18.63 — the next choice matters; BG 2.7 — seeking what is best / turning toward guidance is responsible (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/7/); BG 18.73 — clearer seeing can lead to aligned action (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/73/).
- **Compassionate response:** Pause; remember the teaching meaning; choose a repair — brief apology, restored tone, clear next step — without forcing the child to comfort the parent.
- **Family action:** Count repair-after-snap as a valid weekly practice success; keep details private.
- **What not to say:** Forced public apology theater; using the child’s feelings as a prop before other families.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — “sorry + kind voice.” Older — distinguish feeling, choice, and repair.
