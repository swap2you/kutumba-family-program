## King Nṛga — teaching paraphrase (no invented dialogue)

King Nṛga, son of Ikṣvāku, was famous for extraordinary charity, especially gifts of cows to brāhmaṇas. On one occasion a cow belonging to a first-class brāhmaṇa wandered into Nṛga’s herd. Unaware of the ownership, Nṛga gave that cow in charity to a different brāhmaṇa. When the first owner and the recipient argued, they approached the king. Nṛga tried to settle the matter with a very large compensatory offer, but the conflict remained unresolved. After death he experienced a severe reaction and later was delivered by the Lord, who also instructed others about the danger of taking a brāhmaṇa’s property, even unknowingly.

## Teaching use

- Good intention does not erase the need for careful method.
- Unresolved wrongs still matter.
- Accountability and repair belong inside a life of piety.
- Connect to BG 3.9: action binds or frees according to purpose and purity of engagement — not slogans alone.
