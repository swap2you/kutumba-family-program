# C2-W1 Case Studies

Fictional constructed cases for classroom use. Anonymous. Not real cohort families.

---

## Case 1 — The ten-second text

**Concrete situation:**  
After a missed pickup, Priya sends a sharp group-chat message blaming her spouse for “always doing this,” copying two relatives. The scheduling issue was real; the public heat was extra.

**Mistaken conclusion:**  
“It was only words. Karma is about big sins, not text messages.”

**Principle:**  
Speech is action. Intention (punish/vent) and method (public haste) shape consequence (trust damage, widened conflict). Work done for separate material interest — here, winning face — binds.

**Source:**  
BG 3.9 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/9/  
BG 4.17 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/17/ (understand action carefully)

**Compassionate response:**  
Acknowledge the legitimate logistics need; apologize for public sharpness; move the repair to a private conversation.

**Action:**  
Apply Before–During–After to the next difficult message: before (purpose = clarity), during (private, short, kind), after (ask if repair is needed).

**What not to say:**  
“Your marriage problems are past-life karma.” “You deserved that reply.”

**Optional age adaptation:**  
Younger — hot words vs soft words as seed pictures. Older — fill intention / method / consequence boxes for the chat scenario.

## Case 2 — The gift that waited for applause

**Concrete situation:**  
Arjun donates an expensive item for a program and repeatedly steers conversation toward who noticed, who thanked him, and whether a photo was posted.

**Mistaken conclusion:**  
“Generosity always frees me from bondage.”

**Principle:**  
External giving with selfish attachment can still bind. BG 3.9 asks for action aimed at the Lord’s satisfaction without selfish attachment.

**Source:**  
BG 3.9 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/9/  
BG 3.13 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/13/ (self-centered vs offered orientation)

**Compassionate response:**  
Affirm the impulse to give; separate love of service from hunger for status.

**Action:**  
One smaller help this week done quietly; practice gratitude without announcement.

**What not to say:**  
“You’re fake.” “People who want thanks are demons.”

**Optional age adaptation:**  
Younger — draw giving with a smile vs giving while pointing at oneself. Older — rewrite the gift with offered intention and no applause demand.

## Case 3 — The late-night repair

**Concrete situation:**  
Meera stays up late repairing her child’s torn poster so the child can present calmly. The child says “okay” and goes to sleep. Meera feels unseen and begins a guilt lecture at breakfast.

**Mistaken conclusion:**  
“If I am not thanked, the service failed — so I should stop helping.”

**Principle:**  
Work done for the Lord’s purpose can be performed without selfish attachment. Recognition is not the certificate of purity.

**Source:**  
BG 3.9 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/9/  
BG 4.18 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/18/ (seeing non-binding quality within work)

**Compassionate response:**  
Name tiredness privately; affirm the child; release the demand for praise; offer the effort to Kṛṣṇa.

**Action:**  
One repair or help act with a silent before-line: “For Your pleasure; I will not demand payment in praise.”

**What not to say:**  
“After all I do for you…” as spiritual teaching. “Ungrateful children create bad karma” as a threat.

**Optional age adaptation:**  
Younger — help a toy and practice not asking for a clap. Older — journal one duty done as offering.
