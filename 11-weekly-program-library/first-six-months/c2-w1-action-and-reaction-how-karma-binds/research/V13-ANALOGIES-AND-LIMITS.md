## 1) Seed and fruit

**Analogy:** Choices are like seeds. Seeds tend toward fruits — sometimes quickly, sometimes after delay. Kind careful service tends toward trust; harsh haste tends toward broken trust.

**Teaching use:** Excellent for K–2 memory phrase and for older delayed-consequence discussion.

**Limits:**
- Not every hardship is a fruit you can name.
- Do not diagnose another person’s illness or poverty as a specific past seed.
- Delayed result is not permission for cruelty now.
- Analogy supports pedagogy; it is not a śāstra quotation.

## 2) Debt ledger

**Analogy:** Actions write entries in a moral ledger. Forgetting an entry does not erase it; repair and purified work address the field of reaction.

**Teaching use:** Helps adults see why “it was only a small thing” can still matter.

**Limits:**
- The Lord is not a cold accountant.
- Bhakti is not spreadsheet therapy.
- Mercy and guidance remain real; do not reduce devotion to bookkeeping.
- Do not use ledger talk to shame children.

## 3) Cooking with offering vs cooking without offering

**Analogy:** Same kitchen labor can free or bind depending on purpose — cooking as offering versus cooking only for isolated sense pleasure (supported pedagogically by BG 3.13).

**Teaching use:** Makes BG 3.9 concrete for household life; pairs with Before–During–After in the kitchen or snack table.

**Limits:**
- Do not shame newcomers’ kitchens or demand perfection.
- A verbal slogan after careless work does not automatically purify negligence.
- Offering includes intention, care, and gratitude — not stickers alone.
- Food rules beyond this week’s scope stay with local temple guidance.

## Quick facilitator cue

State the analogy in one sentence, then state the limit in one sentence, every time.
