## Objective

Students explain, calmly and without scare tactics: **Birth and death are changes of dress for the eternal self**, anchored in **BG 2.22** (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/), and reject the fatalism that “nothing matters if we reincarnate.”

## Essential question

How do we speak calmly about birth and death?

## Teacher background (2 minutes private prep)

- Primary: BG 2.22 — garment analogy for transmigration.  
- Supports (recognition, not new lectures): BG 2.13, 2.20, 2.27; BG 8.5–8.6 for remembrance direction; BG 14.14–15 recognition only (modes arrive C2-W4).  
- KUTUMBA teaching meaning (not a BBT translation label): As a person puts on new garments, giving up old ones, the embodied self similarly accepts new bodies, giving up old ones — taught calmly, without fear tactics for children.  
- Block: scare pedagogy; diagnosing a deceased person’s destination; ghost speculation; “closet shopping” for the next body; reincarnation used to excuse present duty.  
- Privacy: no forced disclosure of personal grief; anonymous questions only.

## Primary text observation (10 min)

Open https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/. Students write on the observation half-sheet:

1. Who is teaching whom in Bhagavad-gītā Chapter 2 (keep non-graphic)?  
2. Which comparison (garments) does the verse use?  
3. One phrase that signals continuity of the embodied self.  
4. One-sentence paraphrase in their own words (not a long dump).  
5. One thing this verse does **not** say (examples: does not say we shop for bodies; does not say frighten children; does not assign destinations).

Target paraphrase to check against: As a person puts on new garments, giving up old ones, the embodied self similarly accepts new bodies, giving up old ones — taught calmly, without fear tactics for children.

Optional skim only if a student finishes early: https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/ and https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/6/ — note continuity and remembrance direction without expanding into modes.

## Worksheet questions (align with activity pack)

1. State this week’s conclusion in one sentence.  
2. Write the primary scripture reference.  
3. Paraphrase BG 2.22 in your words.  
4. Name one misconception to avoid.  
5. Write one family action for the next seven days that includes bedtime remembrance.  
6. Answer the essential question in two or three calm sentences.

Grade with `activities/OLDER-ANSWER-KEY.md`.

## Scenario cards (use these prompts; full fields live in MAIN + research/CASE-STUDIES.md)

**Card A — Bedtime question.** A younger sibling asks, “What happens when someone dies?” while lights are out. Mistaken path: either silence from fear or a graphic lecture. Better path: two calm sentences + offer to sit with a parent for remembrance.

**Card B — Funeral language.** At a gathering, an adult says the deceased “must have had bad karma.” Mistaken path: diagnose destination. Better path: dignity, grief respect, no private karmic verdicts.

**Card C — Teen fatalism.** “If we just get another body, nothing we do matters.” Mistaken path: excuse duty. Better path: continuity raises the stakes of consciousness and choice; connect lightly to next week’s modes only if asked, then defer.

Pairs: name mistaken conclusion → compassionate response → one action → what not to say.

## Matching puzzle / game (real steps)

1. Print `activities/OLDER-ACTIVITY-PACK.md` section B as two cut columns (Term / Meaning).  
2. Shuffle meaning cards.  
3. Students match in pairs within four minutes.  
4. Check against answer key together once — no ranking of speed.  
5. If time remains, complete scramble section C as a silent desk check.

Terms: *dehī*; garment analogy; remembrance; analogy limit.

## Diagram task

On board or printed frame:

- Worn garment → set aside  
- New garment → accepted  
- Embodied self (*dehī*) → continues  
- **Limit box:** Not choosing from a closet / not ghost speculation / not destination diagnosis  

Draw one arrow showing the week’s conclusion: the self continues; teach calmly.
