## 10–20 minute speaking script (deliverable prose)

> Facilitation note: Speak calmly. Pause for the 20-second pair share. Soften language around death. Do not replace this with a research-file deferral. No graphic imagery. No diagnosing anyone’s next destination.

Friends, welcome to Cycle 2, Week 3: Birth, Death, and Reincarnation.

Some of us come tonight with curiosity. Some come with grief. Some come with children’s bedtime questions that arrive at the wrong hour. We will teach with calm hope. We will not frighten anyone into devotion, and we will not turn this room into a ghost forum.

Our essential question is: How do we speak calmly about birth and death?

Please open with me the primary verse: Bhagavad-gītā 2.22. The stable link is https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/. I will not read a full purport into the room. I will give our KUTUMBA teaching meaning — an original classroom paraphrase, not labeled as a BBT translation:

“As a person puts on new garments, giving up old ones, the embodied self similarly accepts new bodies, giving up old ones — taught calmly, without fear tactics for children.”

Say that back in your own words to the person beside you for twenty seconds — softly.

Here is the conclusion I want every facilitator and parent to be able to repeat: Birth and death are changes of dress for the eternal self — teach without fear.

Notice what the verse is doing. It does not say the person disappears. It does not say the body is the self. It says the embodied self (*dehī*) continues while garments — bodies — are given up and accepted. That is transmigration taught as continuity, not as a horror story.

From Cycle 1 we already practiced: the body changes through childhood, youth, and old age, yet the self continues (BG 2.13 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/). Tonight we take the next step: the same continuity that holds across life stages also holds across births. BG 2.20 steadies us further: the self is not destroyed when the body ends (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/20/). BG 2.27 adds sobriety without scare: birth and death are certain for embodied life (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/27/). We name these calmly the way we name that night follows day — not to terrify children.

Let me make this concrete with worn clothes. When a shirt becomes too small or worn, a person sets it aside and accepts another. The wearer continues. That is pedagogy for continuity. The limit must be said out loud: you do not pick your next body from a closet like shopping. Desire, karma, and the Lord’s arrangement are not a costume rack. Analogies help; they are not the verse.

Second analogy: life-stage photos. A toddler photo, a school photo, a teen photo — faces change; the person is continuous. Limit: photographs do not prove the soul. They only illustrate continuity of identity through change. Śāstra teaches the metaphysics; photos teach calm speech.

Third analogy, especially for younger rooms: a doll changing clothes. The doll remains; outfits change. Limit: dolls are toys; living beings are not dolls. Never let children role-play dying or funerals with the doll.

Now a household picture. Imagine a child at bedtime asks, “Will I die?” The tempting response is either silence that feels like fear, or a frightening lecture about hell and ghosts. We reject both. The compassionate response is: “Your body will change someday, like clothes. You are the person Kṛṣṇa knows. Right now we are safe. Let’s remember Him for one minute and sleep.” That is hope, not scare pedagogy.

## Analogies with limits (inline — teach from here)

### 1. Worn clothes / new garments
- **Teaches:** As worn garments are set aside and new ones accepted, the embodied self continues while bodies change (BG 2.22).
- **Limit (say out loud):** You do not choose the next body from a closet. Analogy ≠ shopping. Desire, karma, and the Lord’s arrangement are not a costume rack.

### 2. Life-stage photos (continuity)
- **Teaches:** Faces and sizes change across photos; the person is continuous — pedagogy for calm speech about change.
- **Limit (say out loud):** Photos do not prove the soul. They illustrate continuity through change; metaphysics rests on śāstra.

### 3. Doll changing clothes (K–2 object lesson)
- **Teaches:** The doll remains while outfits change — gentle entry for young children.
- **Limit (say out loud):** Living beings are not dolls. No funeral role-play. No “dying game.” Stop at continuity and kindness.
