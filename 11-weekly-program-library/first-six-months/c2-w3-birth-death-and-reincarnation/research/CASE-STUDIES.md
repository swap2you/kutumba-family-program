# C2-W3 Case Studies

Three week-specific fictional constructed cases for facilitator practice. Controlling inline copies also live in `teacher/MAIN-FACILITATOR-GUIDE-V12.md`.

### Constructed case 1 — Child bedtime question

- **Situation:** A child at bedtime asks whether they will die tonight and where a deceased grandparent “went,” including whether the grandparent will return as a baby.
- **Mistaken conclusion:** Scare the child into obedience, or invent a confident destination map for the grandparent.
- **Principle:** The self continues through changing bodies; speak calmly; practice remembrance; do not diagnose destinations.
- **Source:** https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/6/
- **Compassionate response:** Affirm the brave question; affirm safety tonight; use clothes language; offer one-minute remembrance and a hug; offer morning follow-up with a trusted adult.
- **Family action:** One-minute bedtime remembrance on three nights (cue after teeth brushing).
- **What not to say:** Threats about dying soon; ghost claims; animal-body guessing; closet-shopping for next lives.
- **Optional age adaptation:** K–2 clothes/doll only; older children add “we don’t know every detail; we trust the Lord.”

### Constructed case 2 — Relative funeral language

- **Situation:** At a memorial, an adult loudly announces the deceased’s precise next destination while another adult jokes about ghosts in front of children.
- **Mistaken conclusion:** Public destination diagnosis equals devotion; ghost talk is harmless entertainment.
- **Principle:** Honor with dignity and remembrance; leave destinations to the Lord; protect children from scare and speculation.
- **Source:** https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/20/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/5/
- **Compassionate response:** Redirect privately; prepare two calm sentences (honor line + child line); offer to step outside with an overwhelmed child.
- **Family action:** Write the two sentences before the next gathering; practice them once at home.
- **What not to say:** Confident destination proclamations; haunting jokes; forcing children to “toughen up” with graphic exposure.
- **Optional age adaptation:** Younger hear only kindness and prayer; older may hear “we can grieve and still trust continuity without guessing chapters.”

### Constructed case 3 — Teen fatalism

- **Situation:** A teen argues that reincarnation means nothing matters and responsibility is pointless.
- **Mistaken conclusion:** Continuity of the self cancels meaning and accountability.
- **Principle:** Continuity increases the weight of consciousness and choice; remembrance and responsibility still matter.
- **Source:** https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/6/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/63/
- **Compassionate response:** Agree that the question is serious; reject fatalism; connect to last week’s responsibility without shame.
- **Family action:** Teen writes “Because I continue, this week I will…” and joins (or witnesses) three nights of one-minute remembrance.
- **What not to say:** Hell threats; “don’t think”; ranking the teen as faithless; “nothing matters until initiation.”
- **Optional age adaptation:** Grades 4–5 use simpler wording: kind choices still matter because the self continues.
