# C2-W3 Analogies and Limits

Use these **inline** in teaching. Always state the limit aloud after the analogy. Analogy serves scripture; it does not replace BG 2.22.

## 1. Worn clothes / new garments (primary pedagogical mirror of BG 2.22)

- **Value:** Bodies are given up and accepted while the embodied self continues — the same comparison the verse uses.  
- **Limit:** This is not shopping for a next body from a closet. Desire, karma, and the Lord’s arrangement are not a costume rack. Do not treat transmigration as a fashion choice.  
- **Classroom line:** “Clothes help us picture change with continuity. They do not let us pick a future body like an outfit.”

## 2. Life-stage photos (continuity through change)

- **Value:** The same person appears across baby / child / youth / elder photos; appearance changes while identity language continues — useful for calm speech about change.  
- **Limit:** Photos do not prove the soul. Metaphysics rests on śāstra (BG 2.13 / 2.22), not album evidence. Do not claim psychology or photography as siddhānta.  
- **Classroom line:** “Photos help us speak calmly about change. They are not ontology proof.”

## 3. Doll or paper-doll clothes change (K–2 object lesson)

- **Value:** Outfit changes; the “friend” remains — a gentle, non-graphic picture of continuity for young children.  
- **Limit:** Living beings are not dolls. No funeral or dying role-play. Stop at continuity and kindness. Never use the doll to invent destinations or animal-body guessing games.  
- **Classroom line:** “Clothes change. Friend stays. We speak softly.”

## Classroom use sequence

1. Teach the analogy in one minute.  
2. Immediately state the limit in one sentence.  
3. Return to the KUTUMBA teaching meaning of BG 2.22.  
4. If a learner presses the analogy further than scripture supports, defer: “That is beyond tonight’s calm teaching; we stay with what the verse says.”
