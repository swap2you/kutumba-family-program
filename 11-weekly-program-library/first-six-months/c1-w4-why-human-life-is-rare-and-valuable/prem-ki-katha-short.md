## 6. Source-grounded narrative

_[Source narrative / paraphrase — not invented dialogue; compressed schedule]_

Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā 24.229–282 tells how Nārada Muni met a hunter who left animals wounded. The hunter identified himself as Mṛgāri and said his father had taught him the practice (CC Madhya 24.242). Nārada explained the consequences of causing suffering, and Mṛgāri asked how he could change.

Nārada told him to break his bow. Mṛgāri worried about his livelihood, but accepted Nārada's assurance and broke it (CC Madhya 24.255–256). Nārada then gave him practical devotional direction: live simply, worship tulasī, chant the holy name, and accept the food that would be supplied. The hunter and his wife accepted only what they needed.

When Nārada returned with Parvata Muni, Mṛgāri carefully moved ants from his path before approaching them (CC Madhya 24.270–272). His conduct now protected the small creatures he once would have ignored. Parvata Muni praised the transforming power of Nārada's association, and Mṛgāri received the Vaiṣṇavas devotionally (CC Madhya 24.278–282).

The story makes this week's primary verse, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.9.29, concrete: rare human life can be redirected through hearing, responsibility, and devotional practice. Families can apply the broken-bow moment proportionately by choosing one harmful habit and one supported step away from it.
