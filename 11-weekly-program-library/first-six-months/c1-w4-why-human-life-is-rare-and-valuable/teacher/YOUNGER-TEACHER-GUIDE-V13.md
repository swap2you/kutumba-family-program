## Objective

Children experience human life as a **rare chance** to ask who we are and serve Kṛṣṇa — with **opportunity language, not fear**. They make a Must / Optional ticket card and protect one tiny practice slot.

## Exact memory phrase

> Human life is a rare chance to ask who I am and serve Kṛṣṇa.

## Teacher background (2 minutes to review)

- **KUTUMBA teaching meaning (adult wording):** After many births one obtains the rare human form — temporary yet able to give the highest purpose. A sober person should quickly endeavor for the ultimate good while this body lasts.
- **Child-facing paraphrase:** Getting a human life is like a special ticket — meant to be used for asking “Who am I?” and serving Kṛṣṇa. We speak of chance and kindness, never scary death pressure.
- **Block misconceptions (child words):**  
  - “Scare kids so they chant.”  
  - “Screens always come first.”  
  - “Busy means successful.”
- **Do not:** invent Nārada–Mṛgāri dialogue; show gore; cite Mṛgāri from SB 6.x or SB 4.8 (source is **CC Madhya 24.229–282** only); mock other life forms.

## Materials

- Paper “rare ticket” props (one demo + one per child later)
- Must jar / Optional jar labels (two small boxes or jars)
- Card stock for Must-ticket cards
- Soft toss object; crayons; line-art if available
- Picture cards: screen-first vs practice-first (gentle, not shaming)

## Room setup

Circle; jars visible; open space for Ticket Walk game.

## Minute-by-minute executable plan

### 0–5 min — Opening and recall

1. Welcome; feet on floor.
2. Hold up rare ticket: “Hard to get — meant to be used, not forgotten in a drawer.”
3. Echo memory phrase three times.
4. Ask: “What is one special thing humans can do with words?” (ask questions / say Kṛṣṇa’s names — accept short answers)

### 5–13 min — Story script (paraphrase only; ~8 minutes)

**Say exactly this spine (do not invent deity dialogue):**

> Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam teaches that after many births, the human form is rare. It does not last forever, but it can help us reach the highest purpose. A sober person uses this chance carefully for the ultimate good.  
> Think of a rare ticket to something wonderful. You would not throw it away. You would use it wisely — without panic, without shouting at friends.  
> In Caitanya-caritāmṛta Madhya chapter 24, Nārada meets a hunter named Mṛgāri. With mercy and teaching, the hunter’s life turns toward care and devotion — even careful steps so tiny creatures are not harmed. We tell this gently, without scary details. The source is Madhya 24 — not other books.  
> At home we protect a little time to ask who we are and serve Kṛṣṇa. Screens can wait for Optional. We never scare children with death talk to make them practice.

**Story boundaries:** ŚB 11.9.29 + CC Madhya 24.229–282 paraphrase only. No gore. Opportunity language only. BG 2.40 may be whispered as “a little sincere practice is not lost” if a child fears missing out.

### 13–16 min — Three wonder questions

1. Is human life a rare chance or a scare story? (**Chance**)
2. What belongs in the Must jar first — practice or endless screens? (**Practice / asking who I am**)
3. How did mercy help Mṛgāri’s life change — fear or kind teaching? (**Kind teaching / mercy**)

### 16–24 min — Movement game: Ticket Walk (steps)

1. Children walk in a circle holding imaginary tickets.
2. Teacher calls **Must!** — freeze; whisper memory phrase; take one step toward Must jar.
3. Teacher calls **Optional!** — soft sit; count to three; then stand (Optional waits).
4. Teacher calls **Kind chance!** — hand on heart; smile (no fear faces).
5. Three rounds. Round three: point to practice-first card, not screen-first.
6. End seated; echo phrase once.

### 24–32 min — Craft: Must-ticket card (steps)

1. Give ticket-shaped card stock.
2. Front: decorate “RARE TICKET” / draw a small path or lamp.
3. Inside: write memory phrase (helper may write) or short: *Rare chance — ask & serve.*
4. Back: two boxes — **Must** (draw hearing / memory phrase) and **Optional** (draw a toy/screen).
5. Child places a sticker or star in Must.
6. Practice: “Must first, then Optional.”

Full steps in `activities/v13-younger/`.

### 32–35 min — Printable / coloring task

Line-art if time; else keep ticket.

### 35–38 min — Behavior redirects and quiet reset

- **Fear talk from peer** — “We use chance words, not scare words.”
- **Running** — “Ticket walk.”
- **Snatching** — “Kind hands.”
- **Quiet reset** — calm corner with ticket; rejoin on Must.

### 38–40 min — Cleanup and parent handoff

> Memory: “Human life is a rare chance to ask who I am and serve Kṛṣṇa.” Home cue: one Must slot before Optional screens. No death scare. Mṛgāri source if asked: CC Madhya 24.

## Behavior redirects (quick reference)

| Signal | Teacher line | Child action |
|---|---|---|
| Scare language | “Chance words only.” | Soft rewrite |
| Screen demand | “Must first.” | Point to Must |
| Running | “Ticket walk.” | Walk |
| Shutdown | “Quiet reset okay.” | Calm corner 60 sec |

## Backup low-prep

Soft toss: catch → say “chance,” “ask,” “serve,” or “Must.” Three phrase echoes. Optional: sort picture cards into Must vs Optional jars.

## What success looks like

- Child can say rare chance (not scare).
- Child can echo memory phrase.
- Child can point Must before Optional.

## Boundaries

- Snack/water at 3:30; no weekly meal.
- Mṛgāri = **CC Madhya 24 only**.
- No species contempt; no panic harvest language.
