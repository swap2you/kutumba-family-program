**Purpose:** Clarify changing influences without replacing śāstra. Every analogy carries an explicit limit.

| Analogy | How it helps | Explicit limit | Classroom cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Three weather faces** (clear / windy / fog) | Children can match calm, restless, and dull *atmospheres* without calling a classmate a mode. | Weather is a metaphor. Guṇas are ontological features of material nature per BG 14.5—not meteorology. | “Name the weather of the room, not the person.” |
| **Colored filters on a lamp** | Same awareness looks different through clarifying, agitating, or darkening tints—modes condition perception. | Filters are external props we control in a demo. Modes are not fully conquered by DIY willpower; BG 14.26 points to devotion. | Show filter; remove filter; refuse “I fixed my guṇas with crafts.” |
| **Three evenings in one home** | Same eternal souls, different evenings—blocks fixed labels and supports environment redesign. | A calm evening is not proof of liberation; it can host pride (BG 14.6). A dull evening may involve exhaustion—never diagnose. | Use three tableau cards side by side. |

## Facilitator reminders

- Prefer analogies that describe **conditions**.
- After each analogy, restate BG 14.5 teaching meaning in one sentence.
- If an analogy starts driving the theology, stop and return to the verse URL.
