## Example 1 — Battlefield instruction context of Bhagavad-gītā Chapter 14

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Traceable source | Bhagavad-gītā Chapter 14 opening through 14.5ff — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/5/ |
| What can be said | In the Gītā’s instruction setting, Kṛṣṇa explains to Arjuna that the three modes born of material nature condition the eternal living being in the body. The teaching is systematic: naming the modes, describing how each binds, and later pointing to devotion as the means that transcends them (see also BG 14.26). |
| Teaching use | Authority rests on śāstra pedagogy to a perplexed warrior-disciple—not on modern typing of personalities. |
| Limitation | Do not invent dramatic speeches, battlefield sound effects, or “Arjuna said…” lines beyond the verse sense. Keep child delivery calm. |

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Traceable sources | https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/6/ and https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/26/ |
| What can be said | The same chapter that praises goodness as illuminating also warns that goodness binds by happiness and knowledge-attachment; the chapter’s answer for transcending the modes is unfailing devotion to the Lord—not congratulating oneself for a tidy room. |
| Teaching use | Direct antidote when adults begin ranking other families. |
| Limitation | Historical biography of a modern person is not required for this point; stay with śāstra pairing. |
