# C2-W4 Case Studies

**Week-specific fictional household cases for facilitation.**  
**Rule:** Describe conditions and choices. Never label a person as a mode.

---

## Case 1 — Rushed multi-screen evening

- **Concrete situation:** Dinner ends. One television stays on “for background,” a tablet plays a second show, a parent answers late work chat, and a child opens homework then drifts to the louder screen. Voices sharpen over “just one more episode.” The planned five-minute reading never starts.
- **Mistaken conclusion:** “This is who we are—high-energy people. Modes language doesn’t apply to modern life.”
- **Principle:** Passion-like restlessness binds through craving and unfinished stimulation; BG 14.5 frames modes as conditioning influences on the embodied self, not as optional personality flavor.
- **Source:** BG 14.5; BG 14.7; BG 14.9 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/5/
- **Compassionate response:** “Attention is split. Let’s change the room before we blame each other.”
- **Family action:** Five-minute Clarity Before Chanting reset—silence devices, clear one table surface, wash hands, sit, chant briefly.
- **What not to say:** “You’re so rajasic.” “Devotees don’t have phones.”
- **Optional age adaptation:** Younger practice soft-voice freeze; older write one remove and one add.

## Case 2 — Neglected dull evening

- **Concrete situation:** Dishes stack into a second day. Curtains stay closed after sunset. Everyone says they are too tired for practice, yet scrolling continues without a chosen bedtime. Starting chanting feels “pointless tonight.”
- **Mistaken conclusion:** “That kid is tamas. Nothing will change until *they* change.”
- **Principle:** Ignorance-like covering shows as neglect, unclear starts, and inertia (BG 14.8; 14.17). Still speak about conditions. The eternal self is not identical to tonight’s fog.
- **Source:** BG 14.5; BG 14.8; BG 14.17 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/8/
- **Compassionate response:** “Heavy evenings need a small door, not a lecture.”
- **Family action:** Reset one square meter, turn on a lamp, drink water, recite the memory line once, sleep logistics handled privately by parents.
- **What not to say:** “Lazy tamasic.” Public mental-health diagnosis in circle.
- **Optional age adaptation:** Younger tidy-three-toys game; older list one covering cue and one clarifying cue.

## Case 3 — Clean calm evening used to judge others

- **Concrete situation:** The living room is ordered, the family chanted on time, and a parent feels peaceful—then remarks that another family’s messy pickup area means they are “in ignorance,” or critiques a child’s packaged snack as “tamasic food.”
- **Mistaken conclusion:** “Our sattva proves we are ahead.”
- **Principle:** Goodness still binds by happiness and knowledge-pride (BG 14.6). Transcendence is linked to unfailing devotion (BG 14.26). Food verses (BG 17.8–10) speak tendencies, not police power over allergies or budgets.
- **Source:** BG 14.6; BG 14.26; BG 17.8 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/26/
- **Compassionate response:** “Clarity is for hearing Kṛṣṇa, not scoring neighbors.”
- **Family action:** Replace the judgment sentence with gratitude or service; keep resets private; stop food commentary in the group.
- **What not to say:** “They’re a tamasic family.” Allergy/budget shame.
- **Optional age adaptation:** Children practice “calm helps me hear” without naming classmates; older students write a BG 14.26 redirect sentence.
