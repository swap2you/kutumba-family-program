## Two-minute summary

Material nature produces three modes—goodness (*sattva*), passion (*rajas*), and ignorance (*tamas*). These modes condition the eternal embodied self within the body. They mix and change; they are influences, not fixed personality labels. Families learn to notice which mode is shaping a moment, design small environmental supports for clarity, and keep bhakti—not pride—as the goal. Primary: **BG 14.5** (https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/5/). Lab: **Clarity Before Chanting**. Home: one five-minute environment reset before family chanting or reading.

## Core teaching script (10–20 minutes — speak, do not summarize only)

### Beat 1 — Three evenings in one home (2–3 min)

Invite the room to picture the *same* household on three different evenings—not three different “types of people.”

1. **Calm/ordered evening:** room reasonably clear, meal roughly on time, voices lower, a short reading feels possible.
2. **Rushed multi-screen evening:** several devices active, plans changing, voices sharp, attention fragmented.
3. **Neglected dull evening:** dishes linger, sleep is irregular, starting anything feels heavy, clarity feels far away.

Say clearly: “These are **conditions**. The people are still eternal souls. Modes shape the moment; they are not permanent name-tags.”

### Beat 2 — Primary verse layers (3–4 min)

Read or display Devanāgarī and IAST briefly. State the KUTUMBA teaching meaning again. Emphasize three load-bearing words from the teaching:

- **Born of material nature** — the modes are *prakṛti’s* products, not the soul’s identity.
- **Bind** — they condition perception, desire, and action.
- **Eternal embodied self** — the living being remains *avyaya* (imperishable) even while conditioned in the body.

Then paraphrase BG 14.6–14.9 without purport dumps:

- **Sattva (goodness):** clarifying, illuminating; yet can bind through attachment to comfort and “I know / I am better.”
- **Rajas (passion):** restless wanting and doing; binds through attachment to results and endless projects.
- **Tamas (ignorance):** covering, dulling; binds through neglect, confusion, and inertia.

Use BG 14.17 in one sentence: knowledge tends to grow from goodness; greed from passion; foolishness and illusion from ignorance. Use BG 14.18 only as a recognition map of *tendencies*, not a threat chart for children.

### Beat 3 — Mixing, change, and the essential question (2–3 min)

Modes rarely arrive as pure colors. Fatigue, media, company, time of day, and food *tendencies* can tilt a room within hours. Therefore the week’s practice question is always local and humble: **Which mode is shaping this moment?**—not “What mode *are you*?”

Name favorable habits for bhakti: order that serves attention, moderated stimulation, timely rest, gentle speech, and a short clear space before the holy name. Clarify: external clarity supports hearing; it is not itself pure devotion.

### Beat 4 — Goodness trap and BG 14.26 (2–3 min)

Warn: a clean room plus a critical heart is still bondage. BG 14.6 already warns that goodness binds by happiness and knowledge-attachment. Connect immediately to BG 14.26: one who serves the Lord with unfailing devotion transcends the modes. Environment design is a servant of bhakti, not a scoreboard for superiority.

### Beat 5 — Food note with hard boundary (1 min)

If food arises via BG 17.8–10, speak only in tendencies (fresh/clarifying vs craving-heavy vs stale/neglectful leanings). Explicitly refuse: allergy shaming, medical-diet commentary, budget judgment, and child food policing in circle.

### Beat 6 — Bridge to lab and home (1–2 min)

Preview **Clarity Before Chanting**: five minutes—put away three items, wash hands, silence devices, sit, then chant briefly. Home minimum is the same reset once this week before family chanting or reading.

## Analogies with limits (≥3)

### 1) Three weather faces

- **Value:** Clear sky / windy storm / dark fog helps children and adults name calm, restless, and dull *atmospheres* without naming people.
- **Limit:** Weather is impersonal meteorology. Modes are ontological features of material nature per śāstra. Do not claim the analogy “proves” the guṇas.

### 2) Colored filters on a lamp

- **Value:** Same light (awareness) looks different through yellow, red, or dark filters—modes tint perception and response.
- **Limit:** Filters are chosen objects. Modes are not toys we fully control by willpower alone; bhakti (BG 14.26) is the crossing, not clever filter-swapping as a personality hack.

### 3) Three evenings in one home

- **Value:** Same family, different conditioning—supports anti-labeling.
- **Limit:** Do not romanticize the “calm evening” as spiritual arrival. Calm can host pride. Neglect can also hide depression or exhaustion—never diagnose; stay pastoral and practical.

## Three constructed family cases

### Case 1 — Rushed multi-screen evening

- **Concrete situation:** After dinner, two screens play different videos, a parent answers work messages, a child starts homework then stops to watch, voices rise over “five more minutes,” and planned reading never begins.
- **Mistaken conclusion:** “We’re just a passionate family—that’s our personality.”
- **Principle:** Passion-like restlessness binds through attachment to stimulation and unfinished craving (BG 14.7; 14.9); modes shape the *moment*, not a fixed family identity (BG 14.5).
- **Source:** BG 14.5; BG 14.7; BG 14.9 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/5/
- **Compassionate response:** “The room is loud and split. Let’s name the condition, not blame one person.”
- **Action:** Five-minute Clarity Before Chanting reset: devices silent, three items away, seats set, three minutes response chanting.
- **What not to say:** “You’re so rajasic.” “Real devotees never use phones.”
- **Optional age adaptation:** Younger—hold up the “windy” weather face and practice freeze-and-soft-voice. Older—write one remove / one add for the evening.

### Case 2 — Neglected dull evening

- **Concrete situation:** Dishes from yesterday remain, laundry sits in the doorway, everyone overslept feelings, and the family says “we’re too tired for anything” while scrolling without choosing rest or practice.
- **Mistaken conclusion:** “Someone in this house *is* tamas—especially that teenager.”
- **Principle:** Ignorance-like covering shows as neglect, inertia, and unclear starts (BG 14.8; 14.17); still describe conditions, not persons (verse-pack scope boundary).
- **Source:** BG 14.5; BG 14.8; BG 14.17 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/8/
- **Compassionate response:** “Dullness is heavy. We can change one square meter and one decision.”
- **Action:** Ten-minute micro-reset of one surface + lights on + water + sit for one verse memory line; sleep plan deferred to parents privately if needed.
- **What not to say:** “You’re lazy/tamasic.” Public diagnosis of depression or character attack.
- **Optional age adaptation:** Younger—tidy three toys into a basket as a game. Older—identify one “covering” cue (dark room, no start time) and one clarifying cue.

### Case 3 — Clean calm evening used to judge others

- **Concrete situation:** The home is tidy, incense is lit, chanting happened on time. A parent then comments that another Kutumba family “must be in ignorance” because their house looked messy at pickup, or critiques a child’s school lunch as “tamasic.”
- **Mistaken conclusion:** “Our sattva means we are spiritually ahead.”
- **Principle:** Goodness still binds by happiness and knowledge-pride (BG 14.6); transcending the modes is linked to unfailing devotion, not interior-design victory (BG 14.26). Food talk stays at tendency level without policing (BG 17.8–10 carefully).
- **Source:** BG 14.6; BG 14.26; BG 17.8 — https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/26/
- **Compassionate response:** “Clarity is a gift for hearing—gratitude, not ranking.”
- **Action:** Replace judgment sentence with one service sentence; keep home reset private; if food anxiety appears, stop circle commentary.
- **What not to say:** “They’re a tamasic family.” “That lunch is sinful.” Allergy or budget shame.
- **Optional age adaptation:** Younger—practice saying “calm helps me hear” without naming classmates. Older—write how BG 14.26 redirects pride to bhakti.

## Five likely questions with source-based answers

**Q1: Are the modes scientifically proven?**  
**A:** The guṇas as ontological features of material nature are taught by śāstra (BG 14.5ff). Empirical studies may describe attention, stress, and interruption costs in modern environments, but they do not prove or disprove the metaphysical guṇas. Keep doctrine on scripture; keep science in the helper lane for habits only.

**Q2: Can I tell my spouse/child “you’re in tamas”?**  
**A:** No. The verse pack boundary forbids mode-labeling as shame. Describe a condition (“the room is chaotic; let’s reset”) and choose an elevating action. BG 14.5 addresses binding influences on the embodied self—not a license for domestic ranking.

**Q3: If we clean up and eat “sattvic,” are we liberated?**  
**A:** No. BG 14.6 warns goodness still binds; BG 14.26 points to unfailing devotion as transcending the modes. Clean order serves hearing; it is not the destination.

**Q4: How should we use BG 17.8–10 on foods?**  
**A:** Only as general tendency language. Never police allergies, medical needs, or budgets in class or at home under “mode” language. Compassion outranks commentary.

**Q5: What do we do with BG 14.18’s upward/middle/downward language around children?**  
**A:** Recognition only, calmly. Do not scare children with destiny charts. Keep child pedagogy on habits that help hearing and kindness.

## Common misconceptions

- Modes are fixed personality types or Enneagram-like identity labels.
- Calling someone a mode is “honest spiritual feedback.”
- Sattva equals pure devotion / liberation.
- Messy house = bad devotee; tidy house = advanced devotee.
- Food rules override medical, allergy, or economic reality.
- Noticing modes means gossiping about other families’ “levels.”
- Environmental tips replace surrender and chanting (imports C2-W5 poorly as technique-only).

## Bhakti laboratory — Clarity Before Chanting

1. Show contrast: cluttered noisy setup vs simple clear setup (30–60 seconds).
2. Families (or demo family) do a **five-minute reset**: put away items, wash hands, silence devices, arrange seating.
3. Lead about three minutes of attentive mahā-mantra response chanting.
4. Reflect: which physical changes altered attention?
5. Clarify: external goodness supports bhakti; it is not itself pure devotion (BG 14.6 + 14.26).

**Competency check:** Participant can name three modes + one tendency each, and can analyze a condition without labeling a person.
