## Objective

Students observe BG 8.15, distinguish temporary contact pleasure from lasting shelter in the Lord, refuse “affection is worthless,” complete a Temporary→Gratitude→Lasting diagram, and answer: **How can enjoyment become gratitude and service?**

## Exact memory phrase

> Temporary joys can be used with gratitude; lasting fulfillment is in Kṛṣṇa.

**Short echo:** Enjoy with thanks; shelter in Kṛṣṇa.

## Teacher background

- **KUTUMBA teaching meaning:** Having attained the Lord, great souls do not return again to this temporary world of misery; they have reached the highest perfection. (KUTUMBA teaching meaning — not BBT translation.)
- **Supporting frames:** BG 5.22 — sense-contact pleasures are temporary (support only); BG 9.27 — ordinary acts can turn toward offering (support only). **Neither replaces BG 8.15 as locked primary.**
- **Block:** “Material things and family affection are worthless”; “the next purchase will finally satisfy”; “science proves detachment / proves Kṛṣṇa.”
- **Tone lock:** “Temporary world of misery” does not authorize shaming birthdays, toys, or hugs.

## Minute-by-minute plan

### 0–5 min — Settle and hook

1. Welcome; feet on floor.
2. Hook: “Can something be enjoyable and still not be final shelter?” Collect two answers.
3. Essential question: How can enjoyment become gratitude and service?

### 5–14 min — Primary text observation (BG 8.15)

1. Display verse; read IAST aloud together once; Devanāgarī slowly or authorized audio.
2. Students underline: **attained the Lord / temporary world of misery / highest perfection**.
3. Observation prompts (write short answers):
   - What is lasting according to the teaching meaning?
   - What must we **not** conclude about hugs, toys, or lawful fun?
   - Confirm locked primary: BG 8.15 (not BG 5.22 / 9.27).
4. Bridges:
   - BG 5.22 — temporary nature of sense-contact pleasure (support).
   - BG 9.27 — offering mood for ordinary acts (support).
5. Paraphrase only; Dhruva mention only with “no identical-result promise.”

### 14–22 min — Worksheet questions

Students complete Section A:
1. Restate teaching meaning in one sentence.
2. Mark locked primary as BG 8.15.
3. List two temporary joys receivable with gratitude.
4. Why “affection is worthless” is not this week’s teaching.
5. Write a gratitude pause before a non-essential purchase.
6. What gratitude research can support — and cannot prove.

Circulate with answer key for directions, not ranks.

### 22–28 min — Scenario cards (small groups)

**Card A — Weekend purchase chase for a happiness spike.** Mistaken: The next thing will finally satisfy.  
**Card B — Parent shames a child’s joy in a toy.** Mistaken: Material joy is sinful; affection worthless.  
**Card C — Work always defeats prayer time.** Mistaken: Career alone is permanent security.

Each group writes mistaken conclusion / principle / compassionate response / better action / what not to say. Share 60 seconds.

### 28–33 min — Puzzle / game: Gratitude Fork

1. Teacher reads short forks (two endings).
2. Teams hold **G** (gratitude/service path) or **S** (seize forever / shame joy).
3. Three rounds; explain one G using BG 8.15 or offering language from BG 9.27.
4. Keep energy respectful; no mocking wrong holds.

Sample forks:
- Grab next buy angrily vs pause and thank
- Force throw-away to “prove detachment” vs enjoy + thanks
- Cancel all livelihood vs protect one cue without quitting-job theater

### 33–37 min — Diagram task

Fill:

`Temporary joy → Gratitude / offering → Lasting shelter in Kṛṣṇa (BG 8.15)`

Circle lasting shelter; write short echo inside. Low-risk examples only (favorite snack, birthday candle) — not forced spending disclosures.

### 37–40 min — Project contribution + reflection + handoff

1. Project: “Before _______________ we will say Thank You, Kṛṣṇa / practice offering mood.”
2. Reflection: How does BG 8.15 challenge both endless acquisition and contempt for affection?
3. Cleanup; reunification sheets ready.
4. Parent handoff: gratitude pause; do not shame toys; gratitude studies ≠ ontology proof.

## Extension (if group finishes early)

One sentence on Dhruva (ŚB 4.8–4.9): mixed motive can turn toward the Lord — do not promise identical results to children.
