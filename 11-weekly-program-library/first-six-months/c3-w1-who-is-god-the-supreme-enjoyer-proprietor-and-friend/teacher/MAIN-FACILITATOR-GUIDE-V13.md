## Core explanation (facilitator must say)

BG 5.29 gives a personal conception of God that produces peace: Kṛṣṇa is the supreme enjoyer of sacrifice and austerity, the Lord of all worlds, and the friend of every living being. Families practice stewardship — caring for entrusted time, body, property, and relationships — without claiming ultimate ownership or neglecting care with a careless slogan. Simple offering mood remembers He enjoys devotion, not our ego performance.

**Conclusion line:** Peace comes from knowing Kṛṣṇa as enjoyer, proprietor, and friend of all.

**Common errors to block:**
1. “I am the ultimate owner/enjoyer.”
2. Neglect of stewardship (careless misuse because “Kṛṣṇa owns it”).

## Analogies / examples with limits (teach inline)

### 1) Peace triangle
- **Say:** Three corners — Enjoyer, Proprietor, Friend — hold a family steady. Remove one corner and the structure wobbles into greed, anxiety, or cold religion.
- **Limit:** Pedagogy diagram, not a verse quotation.
- **Younger:** Three pictures: Kṛṣṇa receives, Kṛṣṇa owns, Kṛṣṇa hugs as friend.
- **Older/adult:** Which corner was missing in a sample conflict?
### 2) Borrowed bicycle
- **Say:** A borrowed bicycle is ridden carefully, returned clean, and never treated as absolute private property for reckless stunts. Stewardship is greater care because it is entrusted.
- **Limit:** Do not shame poverty or wealth. Analogy ≠ śāstra quote.
- **Younger:** Shared basket belongs to Kṛṣṇa; we care for it together.
- **Older/adult:** Name one home item you will treat as entrusted this week.
### 3) Leaf offered with love
- **Say:** BG 9.26 reminds that a simple offering given with devotion is accepted. The center is love and recognition of who enjoys — not expensive display.
- **Limit:** Do not invent ritual rules beyond program scope.
- **Younger:** Offer a drawing or flower with a smile — no performance ranking.
- **Older/adult:** Separate offering mood from impressing adults.

## Three constructed household cases (week-specific)

### Constructed case 1 — Credit fight after seva
- **Situation:** A fictional family finishes a temple cleanup. Two adults argue who “really did the service” and who should be thanked publicly.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** I am the enjoyer of the credit; peace comes from winning recognition.
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 5.29 — Kṛṣṇa is the enjoyer; knowing Him brings peace.
- **Compassionate response:** Thank cooperation; relocate enjoyer to Kṛṣṇa; keep friendship intact.
- **Family action:** Before next shared duty, say the three-line remembrance together.
- **What not to say:** Public ranking of who is more advanced. Shame for wanting thanks.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — offering picture + friend picture. Older — rewrite credit claim.
### Constructed case 2 — Toy / money grab
- **Situation:** Siblings (or adults in a budget talk) escalate “mine” language until trust breaks.
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** I am the ultimate owner; sharing is loss.
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 5.29 proprietor + Īśo 1 allotted use.
- **Compassionate response:** Name entrusted care; practice a stewardship return of one item; keep dignity.
- **Family action:** One daily duty with “Kṛṣṇa owns” remembrance beforehand.
- **What not to say:** Calling a child greedy as identity. Comparing family incomes.
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — shared basket. Older — stewardship vs grab sort.
### Constructed case 3 — Careless ‘Kṛṣṇa owns it’
- **Situation:** Someone breaks or wastes an item and shrugs, “Kṛṣṇa owns everything anyway.”
- **Tempting mistaken conclusion:** Proprietor teaching cancels stewardship.
- **Relevant principle / source:** BG 5.29 + stewardship: knowing the Owner increases care.
- **Compassionate response:** Correct: because He owns it, we care more, not less. Repair if possible.
- **Family action:** Family card: “Kṛṣṇa owns → we care.” One repair or tidy act.
- **What not to say:** Harsh humiliation. Inventing punishments as “bhakti.”
- **Age adaptation:** Younger — return item clean. Older — write care implication of ownership.
